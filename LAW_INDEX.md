## Dissent register

### law.capital-engine

- The window tiers are the most contestable numbers in the whole framework: they are written here as law-like constants but are in fact guesses. A critic who shows that the pharmaceutical tier is too short destroys the engine in the most capital-intensive domain, and the draft has no answer except measurement.
- A buyout formula published in advance is an invitation to game V_expected; the valuation panel is the single point of failure this law depends on, and panels are exactly where capture concentrates.

### law.civil

- CIV4-CIV5 make the deployer strictly liable for agent harm and presume against whoever withheld the logs; this allocates the cost of uncertainty to the party with the information, which is efficient but will be experienced as punitive, and may push deployment into less scrupulous jurisdictions.
- A civil code this short delegates almost everything to tribunals and the bench; critics will say that is not a code but a mood, and they would be right about the drafting risk.

### law.commons

- The reciprocity clause is this law's most aggressive article and its most likely to be captured: a schedule written by incumbents can become a tariff wall in commons clothing. It needs an independent schedule-setter or it will be hated.
- A national dividend paid equally to residents regardless of need spends money on people who do not need it; the floor would do more per unit of money. The dividend's real function is political (making shared ownership visible), and that is exactly what a critic would cut first.

### law.community

- Hard subsidiarity with a 100,000-person threshold will strand some decisions (environmental spillovers, infrastructure corridors) at the wrong level; the escalation rule is meant to catch them, but registers of escalations have a long history of being ignored.
- A fixed local budget share of 35% is a number with no pilot behind it; too high fragments the floor's universality, too low makes the assembly a talking shop with a budget for benches.

### law.constitution

- The steering layer may be capture-prone and slower than markets in fast-moving domains; a 'steer only upstream, never downstream' variant should be drafted and tested.
- A universal dividend may be fiscally unstable if the sovereign fund underperforms; a floor-only variant with no cash dividend should be piloted in parallel.
- Entrenchment of any article risks locking in a mistake; a fully revisable constitution with strong epistemics may be strictly better.

### law.criminal

- A criminal code drafted as a list of harms will be pressed by the next moral panic into adding offences anyway; the anti-symmetry (CRI6 protects disclosed failure) is the only structural brake, and it is a brake made of words.
- Twenty-year maxima will read as softness after the first atrocity; this law bets the floor's universality (CRI5) against the demand for exemplary suffering, and that bet has lost in most democracies.
- Victim restitution first (CRI9) can quietly turn prosecution into a collection agency for the loudest harm, while diffuse harms (environment, fraud on many) get the residue.

### law.currency

- An independent central bank with a formula budget is still a body of experts governing without elections; the sortition chamber has no hook into it, and 'independence' can become unaccountability with better manners.
- Sovereign accounts at the central bank socialise the deposit franchise and could quietly nationalise credit allocation — the very failure CUR7 forbids — one convenience at a time.
- Full convertibility (CUR10) ties this jurisdiction's hands in a crisis; every real currency framework keeps capital tools on the shelf, and pretending otherwise may just mean using them illegally when the day comes.

### law.defense

- A 3% peacetime cap and a 30% civil-defence share describe a nation with forgiving neighbours; a hostile security environment breaks both numbers immediately, and the draft's only honest answer is that this law is written for the pilot, not for wartime.
- Publishing rules of engagement and unit costs gives adversaries real information; the draft bets that accountability is worth more than the marginal operational secrecy, which is a values claim dressed as an efficiency claim.

### law.development

- DEV7 (public money means public technology) may gut university-industry transfer in domains where firms simply refuse public co-funding to avoid day-one release; the bounty is supposed to compensate, but its size is a guess.
- The prohibited-use list in DEV6 is a standing invitation to mission creep: every technology can be argued to have a weapons use. Without a narrow, published list and bench review, this is the weak joint of the whole law.

### law.direction

- Any published mission list is a list that lobbies will try to join, and the admission test may be gamed by writing a problem statement that is really a subsidy request. A stronger variant would allocate mission selection to a sortition panel with no institutional memory, at the cost of expertise.
- Capping the number of missions may be exactly wrong in a period of rapid technological change, when many parallel bets are cheap; the cap assumes attention and stewardship are the scarce resource, which may be false in domains where execution is buyer-limited.

### law.earn-and-pay

- PAY3 (mandatory profit and equity sharing) is the most intrusive article in the engine pillar and the most likely to deter capital; the thresholds are set to bite only above 50 employees precisely because the draft cannot cost the deterrent.
- A wage floor tied to the social floor imports every error of the floor into the cost base of every small employer; a hostile pilot could show job losses concentrated exactly where the floor was meant to help.

### law.education

- EDU2 (framework, not script) is the article most likely to widen variance between schools: autonomy helps strong schools most. The draft answers with foundational minimums and outcome publication, which is a real mechanism but slow.
- A lifelong entitlement of 100 funded hours per adult per year is a round number pretending to be a calculation; no one has measured demand, and if take-up is high the fiscal cost is not in the framework's arithmetic anywhere.

### law.elections

- The double-majority rule plus quorum makes entrenchment nearly reversible-only: a minority of communities can block what a majority of people want, and this law provides no release valve short of constitutional crisis.
- Public-only campaign finance incumbent-protects: challengers depend on the state's own allocation formula, and nothing stops the majority writing rules that quietly starve its critics.
- Sortition's dignity and representativeness are asserted from small-scale trials; at national scale, self-selection into service may skew every cohort toward the confident and the available.

### law.environment

- Boundaries as hard constraints will collide with the floor the first time the cheapest route to floor delivery crosses one; this law resolves that collision in favour of the boundary, and that ordering is a value choice that no evidence can settle.
- The consequence ladder is only as honest as its indicators; a captured statistics office can soften a boundary into a curve, and every downstream 'automatic' trigger becomes advisory wearing a uniform.
- One hundred percent restoration bonds will simply be priced in by large extractors and prohibitive for small ones — concentrating extraction rather than reducing it, which may be the exact opposite of what communities hosting extraction want.

### law.health-care

- The two-tier rule (HLT4) is the article most likely to be quietly broken: a wealthy population will buy speed somewhere, and prohibiting it outright risks driving clinicians abroad while permitting it risks stripping the public queue. The draft tries to police the boundary with waiting-time evidence, which is honest but may be too slow.
- A 7+2 window with a buyout is a bet that pre-commitment can replace the tail of monopoly profit that currently funds drug development; if that bet fails, this law is where the whole framework's engine shows its first crack.

### law.immigration

- A jurisdiction that guarantees the floor to every resident on arrival while keeping entry lanes narrow has simply moved the border into the queue; the queue is where this law's cruelty or its honesty will live, and queues are easier to abuse quietly than borders.
- Five years to citizenship with free preparation still creates two classes of member; every service in this blueprint is rights-based except the vote, which is time-based — a tension the law manages rather than resolves.
- IMM9's pressure review is constitutionalised nativism insurance: it concedes that arrival pressure can legitimately change local politics, which some will read as conceding it can legitimately exclude.

### law.marriage

- MAR6's care-work credit prices care inside a private relationship; a critic will note that this converts love into a ledger at the exact moment it ends, and that the ledger is a poor instrument for what it is measuring.
- Equal treatment of all family forms combined with per-individual floor entitlements creates real arbitrage surface; the draft answers by fixing distortions at the tax rule, which is correct but hard.

### law.social-floor

- The law is written as if the binding constraint were political will rather than arithmetic; a hostile review of FLR4 would show the rent base cannot cover an indexed floor at kappa = 0.4 in a poor jurisdiction, and the draft offers only the band as an answer.
- Unconditionality plus a job guarantee at 1.15x the floor sets a de facto minimum wage that small firms may not be able to bear; the interaction with Earn and Pay Law PAY1 needs explicit costing that this draft does not contain.

### law.tax

- A 4% land charge is a confiscation of capitalized expectations for anyone who bought at pre-announcement prices; Transition Law TRA2 protects paper rights poorly, and this law's compensation answer (transition relief) is the most likely place a rentier revival hides.
- Flat income taxation above a generous allowance is regressive at the top by design; the blueprint bets the progressive work is done by rent taxation and the floor, which only holds if rent collection never erodes — a political assumption, not a mechanical one.
- The data rent article may be unenforceable against platforms that infer the same signals from private exhaust instead of licensed public data.

### law.transition

- The legacy-term cap is the law's one act of retroactivity and its most litigable feature: a right granted for 20 years that is cut to 7 was partially taken, and calling the buyout 'available from day one' does not make the taking voluntary. Expect the first constitutional crisis here.
- Cash-first phasing (TRA5) is a bet that markets and services can absorb cash quickly; if service capacity is the binding constraint, eighteen months of cash with broken clinics is a worse floor than no cash at all.
- A law that sunsets its own transition annually invites endless reopening, and a transition that is perpetually finishing never disciplines anyone — review every year may be too hot, exactly where every other law is too cold.
